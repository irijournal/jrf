# Policy & Strategy Lab
## Assessment Handbook

This handbook contains everything needed to assess the Lab: the components and weights, the gate schedule, the rubrics, the feedback forms, the Case Approval Form, the AI-use policy and the entry and exit diagnostic. It rests on the Learning Outcomes and Competency Framework (Stage 2). Where a decision is still provisional, this handbook says so.

## 1. Principles

1. **Assess thinking through artefacts.** Every mark attaches to something the participant produced.
2. **Keep individual and group performance separate.** Only the 5 per cent pod synthesis mark is shared.
3. **Reward revision.** Gates give feedback; the portfolio is marked once, at the end, with credit for documented revision (provisional decision P-12).
4. **Use one scale everywhere.** Every rubric uses the same four levels, so feedback in Week 1 is directly useful in Week 4.

| Level | Score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Not yet | 0 | Missing, or does not show the competency |
| Foundational | 1 | Correct with a template and prompting; generic; limits unnoticed |
| Working | 2 | Independent, applied to own case, choices justified, main limits named. The working completion standard |
| Advanced | 3 | Tools selected and adapted, objections anticipated, evidence integrated, course changed when evidence required |

## 2. Components and weights

| Component | Weight | Assessed on | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workbook portfolio | 35% | The ten workbooks, with revision notes | After Day 20 |
| Evidence register | 15% | The register, opened on Day 10 | With the client report |
| Pod contribution and peer review | 10% | Pod log, written peer reviews, structured peer assessment | Weekly, totalled at the end |
| Client report | 20% | The final report and annexes | 72 hours after Day 20 |
| Presentation and defence | 20% | Individual pitch and defence (15%); pod synthesis slide (5%) | Day 20 |

**Converting scores to marks.** Each rubric criterion is scored 0 to 3. A component's mark is the sum of its criterion scores divided by the maximum possible, multiplied by its weight. Example: a client report scoring 2, 2, 3, 2, 2, 1 on its six criteria earns 12 of 18, or 13.3 of 20 marks.

## 3. Gates

| Gate | Day | Submission | Feedback returned | Domains in focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Worksheets 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 (revised), revision note, AI disclosure | By Day 7 | A, B, C |
| 2 | 10 | Worksheets 3.1, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 (revised), revision note, AI disclosure | By Day 12 | D, E1 and E2, F |
| 3 | 15 | Worksheets 5.1 to 5.3, 6.1 and the memo, revision note, AI disclosure | By Day 17 | E, F, I1 |
| 4 | 19 | Worksheets 7.1, 7.2, 8.1, 9.1 and the draft deck, revision note, AI disclosure | Before Day 20 (brief) | G, H, I2 |

A pod-checked review of each options appraisal also takes place at the end of Day 17, with one written instructor comment before Day 18 (provisional decision P-19).

**Late submissions.** Gate feedback is formative, so the main cost of lateness is losing feedback in time to use it. Feedback on late gate submissions is given only if time allows. The client report deadline is firm, except for documented emergencies.

## 4. Rubrics

### 4.1 Workbook portfolio (35%)

Score each criterion 0 to 3, using the domain descriptors in the Learning Outcomes and Competency Framework. The tracker codes in brackets point to the commonest weaknesses.

| Criterion | What Working (2) looks like |
|---|---|
| A. Problem framing | Specific, bounded, solution-free statement; a role with authority and a real decision; success defined as a change for people (PF1 to PF3) |
| B. Structuring | MECE tree with stated logic, answerable leaves, pruned with reasons (ST1, ST2) |
| C. Causes and systems | Levels of cause separated; mechanisms stated; a sustaining loop; causal claims qualified (CA1, CA2) |
| D. Institutions | Authority, funding route and delivery chain named; incentives and absent voices identified (IN1, IN2) |
| E. Evidence (in the workbooks) | Several source types; disaggregated analysis; comparative matrix; limits stated (EV1, EV2) |
| F. Hypotheses | Falsifiable hypotheses with disconfirming tests and rivals; honest verdicts; Ledger tags accurate (HY1, HY2) |
| G. Options and choice | Distinct options and a baseline; explicit weighted criteria including distribution; sensitivity tested (DE1 to DE3) |
| H. Delivery | Theory of change with assumptions marked; named owners; justified approach; risk register; outcome indicators (EX1, EX2) |
| J. Revision | Revision notes show substantive changes in response to feedback at every gate (JU1) |

Maximum 27. Domain I (communication) is assessed in the memo inside Workbook 6 under criterion E or F where relevant, and fully in the report and the defence.

### 4.2 Evidence register (15%)

| Criterion | Not yet (0) | Foundational (1) | Working (2) | Advanced (3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Few sources, one type | Several sources, mostly one type | At least three types, covering each priority hypothesis | Sources chosen to test rivals as well as favourites |
| Reliability judgements | None | Labels without reasons | A reason for each rating, drawn from the five questions | Conflicts between sources explained |
| Triangulation | None | Some findings rest on one source | Main findings rest on two independent sources, or say they cannot | Independence of sources assessed, not assumed |
| Accuracy and verification | Errors or invented details | Incomplete citations | Complete Chicago citations, verified against originals | Methods and limits noted for key sources |
| Integrity | AI use undisclosed | Disclosure incomplete | AI use disclosed; every claim traced to a source | |

Maximum 15 (the integrity row is scored 0 to 2, with 2 as its maximum and no Advanced level).

### 4.3 Pod contribution and peer review (10%)

| Criterion | Working (2) looks like |
|---|---|
| Roles | Carried out the weekly pod role (chair, sceptic, evidence checker, scribe), as recorded in the pod log |
| Written peer reviews | Each review names a specific strength, a specific weakness and a specific revision |
| Structured peer assessment | Pod members rate each other's contribution on the peer assessment form; the instructor moderates outliers |

Maximum 9.

### 4.4 Client report (20%)

| Criterion | Working (2) looks like |
|---|---|
| Framing and structure | The first paragraph states the recommendation and the decision; the report is top-down and within the word limit |
| Diagnosis | Causes, institutions and actors explained, with the strongest rival addressed |
| Evidence | Claims at the strength the evidence supports; limitations stated; register consistent with text |
| Choice | Options, criteria, weights, trade-offs and sensitivity explicit; distribution addressed |
| Delivery | Theory of change, owners, approach, risks and indicators coherent with the recommendation |
| Judgement and reflection | What the recommendation gives up, and for whom; the reflection traces changes since the sealed answer to evidence |

Maximum 18.

### 4.5 Presentation and defence (20%)

**Individual (15%).**

| Criterion | Working (2) looks like |
|---|---|
| Pitch | Recommendation in the first minute; five slides in six minutes |
| Slides | Argument titles; honest charts with sources; one message per slide |
| Defence: directness | Answers the question asked |
| Defence: evidence and uncertainty | Cites evidence; concedes uncertainty where warranted; does not overstate |

Maximum 12.

**Pod synthesis (5%).** One shared score, 0 to 3, for an insight that genuinely draws on all the pod's cases about the method, the institutions or the evidence, presented jointly.

### 5.4 Sampling the Analyst's Ledger (competency F4)

At each gate, pick ten substantive claims from across the submission, using the third claim on each of the first ten pages, or every claim if there are fewer than ten. ## 8. AI-use policy

**You may use AI tools to:** suggest branches for an issue tree; propose rival hypotheses; critique a draft argument; find gaps in an analysis; improve the clarity of your own writing; suggest search terms.

**You may not:** use AI output as a source of facts, figures, citations, legal provisions or descriptions of institutions; submit AI-written text as your own analysis; let an AI tool choose your recommendation.

**Every claim must be traced to a source you have read.** AI tools invent plausible citations, figures and quotations. A citation that cannot be verified against the original document is treated as an error (code EV3), whatever its origin.

**Disclose.** Each gate submission and the final report carry a disclosure.

| Tool used | For what | How you checked the output |
|---|---|---|
| | | |
| | | |

Undisclosed use, or presenting AI-generated text or invented evidence as your own work, is a breach of academic integrity.

## 9. Academic integrity and working norms

- Your analysis, writing and recommendation must be your own. Pod members challenge and advise; they do not write each other's work.
- Cite every source you use, including slides, datasets and reports.
- Analyse institutions and incentives, not individuals. Do not attribute failures to named officials.
- Remain non-partisan. Party positions are evidence about political feasibility, not targets.
- Collect no primary data. All evidence is published or publicly available (Decision 5).
- Where a matter is before a court, analyse the policy questions without offering a view on the merits.

## 10. Entry and exit diagnostic

**Task (45 minutes, at home).** Read the case brief. Then write:

1. A problem statement of no more than 150 words.
2. The five questions you would most need answered.
3. One thing the responsible official should do, and why.

**Design safeguards.** Half the cohort takes Brief A at entry and Brief B at exit; the other half the reverse. Scripts are anonymised and mixed before scoring, so the assessor does not know whether a script is from entry or exit.

### Brief A. Water supply in a small town (stylised)

A town of about 150,000 people receives piped water for two hours every second day. Households in higher parts of the town often receive little or none, and many buy water from private tankers at several times the municipal tariff. The municipal council's water section runs the distribution network; a state water board runs the treatment plant and bulk supply. Complaints to the council have risen. Local newspapers report illegal connections and leaking pipes. A state scheme has funded new pipelines in some wards. The council's engineer says the problem is shortage at source; the state board says the problem is losses in the town's network. Councillors want new borewells in their wards.

### Brief B. Pre-school attendance in a rural block (stylised)

In a rural block, children aged three to six are enrolled at anganwadi centres, which provide supplementary nutrition and pre-school education. Records show high enrolment, but visiting officials often find few children present. Anganwadi workers say they spend much of their time on surveys and record-keeping. Some centres operate from rented rooms without space for activities. Parents in some villages send young children to private pre-schools; others keep them at home. The block's child development project officer reports to the district programme officer. A recent state order asks for daily attendance to be uploaded through a phone application.

### Diagnostic rubric

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Precision of the problem statement (A1, A2) | Restates the topic | Names a problem but embeds a cause or solution, or is unbounded | Specific and solution-free; partly bounded | Specific, bounded, about outcomes for people, and names who decides |
| Quality of questions (B1, F1) | Generic | Relevant but overlapping or unordered | Distinct and covering the main areas | Structured, non-overlapping and prioritised |
| Awareness of evidence (E2, E3) | None | Mentions data in general | Names plausible specific sources | Names sources and their limits |
| Actionability (A4, D1, G3) | None, or a slogan | An action without an actor | An actor and an action | An actor, an action, and a reason it is feasible or its main risk |

Maximum 12. The cohort's average change between entry and exit is the programme's first measure of whether it works.

**Suggested certificate wording.** Completed the Policy & Strategy Lab, a 20-day, 40-hour intensive in structured problem-solving for Indian public policy, including an individually assessed policy analysis and defence. Avoid wording that implies a consulting qualification.

## 12. Grading workload

About four hours of feedback and grading per participant across the programme: four gates at about 35 minutes each, six lighter checks at about 10 minutes, and 45 minutes for the report. A single instructor can sustain a cohort of about sixteen; above that, add a teaching assistant. Test these estimates in the pilot and adjust.
