# Policy & Strategy Lab
## Spine Case: Madhyapur

**A stylised case used in every demonstration of the course.** Madhyapur is not a real city. Its institutions follow the real structure of Indian urban governance, and the rules and schemes it refers to are real, but its figures, contracts and records are illustrative. They must never be cited as data about any real place.

## Why a spine case

Every anchor tool in the Lab is demonstrated on the same case, so that participants see one complete project move from a vague concern to a defended recommendation. Framework-by-framework teaching makes it hard to see how the tools connect; a single running case shows it. The finished case also serves as the exemplar portfolio for the cohort.

## The city

Madhyapur is a municipal corporation of about a million people, with residential wards of four broad types: planned colonies, older core wards, informal settlements and new peripheral wards. Household waste is collected door to door by a private contractor under a contract that pays by the tonne collected. Most collection vehicles have a single compartment. The city's landfill site is close to capacity. There is a composting unit working at capacity and no facility for dry waste. Informal waste pickers recover some recyclables from mixed waste and from the landfill.

## The real framework around it

- The Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 place a duty on waste generators, including households, to segregate waste at source into separate streams and hand it over to authorised collectors.
- Solid waste management appears in the Twelfth Schedule to the Constitution (item 6), added by the 74th Amendment, as a function states may devolve to municipalities. Public health and sanitation (State List, entry 6) and local government (State List, entry 5) are State List subjects.
- The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs runs Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0, and Finance Commission grants to urban local bodies are partly tied to sanitation and waste management. Participants should check current guidelines before relying on any detail.
- Cities are ranked annually in the Swachh Survekshan survey.

## The problem and the decision

Most Madhyapur households hand over mixed waste, contrary to the 2016 rules. The Municipal Commissioner must decide, before the next budget, which of three compliance strategies to fund in the coming financial year. The collection contract is due for renewal next year.

## Where the case ends up

The analysis finds that separation fails mainly at collection: households stop separating when they see crews tip separated bags together, and crews mix waste because the contract pays for weight. Distributing bins alone barely changed behaviour, and fines without monitoring raised roadside dumping. The recommendation is a sequence: partner with a waste pickers' cooperative for separated dry-waste collection and ward sorting centres in ten pilot wards now, including informal settlements; then pay for separated collection when the main contract is renewed. The recommendation depends most on the cooperative's capacity to scale, which the pilot is designed to test.

## How to use the case

- Demonstrate each day's anchor tool on Madhyapur before participants apply it to their own cases.
- Use the case to model analytical habits: the illustrative label on every figure, the Analyst's Ledger tags, the sealed Day 3 answer, and the honest verdicts on Day 14 (one hypothesis weakened, one rival supported).
- Point out where the case changes its mind. The sealed answer survives, but the diagnosis absorbs a rival explanation (vehicle compartments), and the recommendation becomes a sequence rather than a single choice.
