## Summary

This document sets out what a participant must be able to do by the end of the Lab, how anyone would know, and where each ability is taught, practised and assessed. It works in five layers:

1. **Ten programme outcomes**: what a graduate can do.
2. **Ten competency domains**: the areas of competence, A to J.
3. **Fifty competencies**: specific abilities, each with an ID (A1, A2 and so on) that workbooks, feedback and rubrics will use.
4. **Performance indicators**: what an assessor observes when a participant meets the Working level, which is the working assumption for completion.
5. **Evidence and assessment**: the artefact that shows the competency and the assessment component in which it counts.

Four checks were run on the framework. Every outcome is taught on at least one named day. Every outcome is assessed in at least two components, at least one of them individual. Every competency has an artefact that shows it. Every domain is assessed formatively before it is assessed summatively. The last check exposed one weakness: the Week 4 domains (option appraisal and delivery design) get only one formative check before the final assessment. Section 13 proposes a fix.

Decision 5 (no primary research) changes the evidence domain. Interviewing has been removed. Qualitative evidence now comes from published sources such as social audit reports, CAG findings, parliamentary committee testimony and field studies, and the Case Approval Form asks for one such source before a case is approved.

## 1. What this framework does

The framework follows the principle of constructive alignment: what participants are meant to learn, what they do in sessions and what is assessed should be the same things, described in the same terms. Most courses that fail do so because these three drift apart. The session teaches one thing, the workbook asks for another and the rubric rewards a third.

Four groups use the framework:

| User | Use |
|---|---|
| Course director | Checks that every day and every workbook earns its place |
| Instructor | Points feedback at a specific competency ID rather than writing general comments |
| Participants | Self-assess against a plain-language version in Workbook 1 |
| Stage 8 (assessment) | Builds rubrics from the domain descriptors in Section 5 |

Outcomes are written with observable verbs. Each one ends in something a participant produces: a statement, a tree, a matrix, a memo, a deck or an answer under questioning. Verbs such as "understand", "appreciate" and "be aware of" are not used, because nobody can observe them.

## 3. Programme learning outcomes

By the end of the programme, a participant who meets the completion standard will be able to:

| # | Outcome | Cognitive level | Main domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Frame.** Convert a broad public concern into a decision-focused problem statement that names the affected population, place, responsible institution, decision-making role, decision and measure of success | Analyse, create | A |
| 2 | **Structure.** Build a mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive issue or hypothesis tree to three levels, and use it to decide what to analyse and what to leave out | Analyse | B |
| 3 | **Diagnose.** Distinguish symptoms, proximate causes and structural causes, and represent at least one feedback relationship that sustains the problem | Analyse | C |
| 4 | **Situate.** Map the jurisdiction, funding, delivery chain and actors that shape the problem, including those with an interest but no voice | Analyse | D |
| 5 | **Hypothesise.** State falsifiable hypotheses, specify what evidence would weaken each one, and keep a register of how each fares | Create, evaluate | F |
| 6 | **Investigate.** Locate, assess and triangulate published Indian administrative, survey, audit and research evidence, recording an explicit reliability judgement for each source | Evaluate | E |
| 7 | **Synthesise.** Reduce findings to a governing message using the Pyramid Principle, keeping facts, inferences and assumptions visibly separate | Evaluate, create | I |
| 8 | **Decide.** Generate at least three genuinely distinct options and appraise them against explicit criteria, including distributional effects and feasibility, to reach a justified recommendation | Evaluate | G |
| 9 | **Plan delivery.** Specify a theory of change, decision rights, responsibilities, risks and indicators for the recommended option | Create | H |
| 10 | **Communicate and defend.** Present the recommendation to a decision-making panel and respond to challenge without overstating the evidence | Evaluate | I, J |

The cognitive levels follow the revised version of Bloom's taxonomy. They rise across the four weeks: Week 1 is mostly analysis, Week 3 adds evaluation of evidence, and Week 4 asks participants to evaluate options and create a plan. Domain J (professional judgement) runs through all ten outcomes rather than belonging to one.

## 5. The competency framework

Each domain has a table of competencies and a three-level descriptor. The indicator column describes what an assessor sees at the **Working** level. The descriptors describe the whole domain at all three levels and are the basis for the Stage 8 rubrics.

### Domain A. Problem framing

Outcome 1. Most weak policy analysis starts with a weak problem statement, and no amount of later rigour repairs it.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Distinguishes problem, symptom, cause and solution | Sorts statements about the case into the four categories and rewrites any problem statement that contains a cause or a solution | 1 | WB1 problem sort | WP |
| A2 | Frames the problem precisely | States the population, place, institution and period, the current and desired condition, and a measure of success that describes a change in what people can do or have | 2 | Problem Definition Canvas | WP (Gate 1), CR |
| A3 | Bounds the scope | Lists at least two things deliberately left out, with reasons, and records at least two ways other actors would frame the problem, stating which framing the analysis adopts | 1, 2 | Canvas | WP |
| A4 | Names the decision and the decision-making role | Identifies a role with the authority to act and a decision that role faces within a stated time, and explains why this role rather than another | 1, 2, 6 | Case Approval Form; canvas | WP, CR |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | The canvas is complete but the statement stays broad or contains a cause or solution. The decision-maker is "the government" in general |
| Working | The statement is specific, free of solutions and bounded. It names a role with authority and a real decision. Success is defined as a change in people's conditions |
| Advanced | Also anticipates how others would frame the problem, explains why the chosen framing serves the decision, and revises the framing when later evidence requires it |

### Domain B. Problem structuring

Outcome 2. Structure decides what gets analysed, and therefore what gets missed.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Applies MECE | Branches at each level do not overlap and together cover the question. The participant can name the logic of each split, for example by stage of delivery, by actor, or by supply and demand | 3 | Issue tree | WP |
| B2 | Builds an issue tree to three levels | By the third level, the tree reaches questions that available evidence could answer | 3, 5 | Issue tree, first and revised versions | WP (Gate 1) |
| B3 | Writes a working answer early | Produces a hypothesis-led version of the tree and a sealed working answer of no more than 100 words | 3 | Sealed answer | CR (reflection) |
| B4 | Prunes the tree | Rates branches by size of effect and ability to influence, drops low-priority branches with written reasons, and names the two or three branches the analysis will pursue | 8 | Pruning matrix | WP (Gate 2) |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Branches overlap or leave out obvious categories. Levels mix questions with answers. The logic of the splits is unclear. Nothing is pruned |
| Working | MECE at every level, with the logic stated. Reaches answerable questions. Pruned with reasons |
| Advanced | Chooses the split that best serves the decision, uses a driver tree where the outcome can be broken down arithmetically, and restructures the tree as evidence arrives |

### Domain C. Causal and systems analysis

Outcome 3. A recommendation is only as good as the causal account behind it.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Separates symptoms, proximate causes and structural causes | Builds a problem tree that places each element at the right level and states the mechanism behind each link | 4 | Problem tree | WP |
| C2 | Uses root-cause tools with care | Uses Five Whys or a fishbone to generate candidate causes, then checks each against the problem tree instead of treating the chain as proof | 4 | WB2 worksheet | WP |
| C3 | Represents feedback | Draws a causal loop diagram with at least one reinforcing or balancing loop that plausibly sustains the problem, with polarities marked | 5 | Causal loop diagram | WP (Gate 1) |
| C4 | Anticipates unintended consequences | For the obvious fix, identifies at least two plausible unintended effects and the mechanism behind each | 5, 16 | Unintended consequences check | WP, PD |
| C5 | Distinguishes correlation, causation and mechanism | Whenever an association is cited, states whether it supports a causal claim and why | 4, 12, 14 | Ledger; data worksheet | WP, ER |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Lists causes without distinguishing levels. Treats one cause as the explanation. Draws arrows without mechanisms |
| Working | Distinguishes levels, states mechanisms, shows a sustaining loop and notes where causation is assumed rather than shown |
| Advanced | Weighs rival causal accounts, identifies the best- and worst-supported links, and uses the systems view to explain why earlier interventions failed |

### Domain D. Strategic and institutional analysis

Outcome 4. This is where the Lab differs most from a consulting course: the state is not a firm.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Establishes jurisdiction | Identifies the list in the Seventh Schedule, or the Eleventh or Twelfth Schedule, that the subject falls under, and which body holds legislative authority, administrative control and funding | 6 | Delivery chain and funding map | WP |
| D2 | Follows the money | Identifies the scheme or budget head that funds the activity, the cost-sharing arrangement and the most recent allocation, with source and year. State-level figures are acceptable where local figures are not published | 6 | Funding map; evidence register | WP, ER |
| D3 | Maps the delivery chain | Maps each link from policy-maker to frontline worker to citizen, and marks the link where the evidence suggests delivery breaks down | 6, 7 | Delivery chain map | WP, CR |
| D4 | Analyses actors and incentives | Builds a power-interest map that includes at least one group with high interest and little power or voice, and states the incentive and likely response of each key actor | 7 | Stakeholder and incentive matrix | WP, CR |
| D5 | Uses context analysis selectively | Uses PESTLE as a check on the issue tree and adds only factors that would change the analysis | 6 | Completeness check | WP |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Describes institutions in general terms. Lists stakeholders without incentives. Gives no account of funding |
| Working | Names the authority, the funding route and the delivery chain. Identifies incentives and absent voices. Locates the probable break point |
| Advanced | Explains how jurisdiction, money and incentives interact to produce the problem, and uses this to judge which options are feasible before appraising them |

### Domain E. Research and evidence

Outcome 6. All evidence is published or publicly available (Decision 5). The skill is finding it, judging it and combining it honestly.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Turns hypotheses into a workplan | Links each priority hypothesis to an analysis, a named source and a deadline | 10 | Workplan matrix | WP (Gate 2) |
| E2 | Locates credible Indian sources | For each priority hypothesis, finds and records at least one official dataset, one audit or parliamentary source, and one independent research source | 10, 11 | Evidence register | ER |
| E3 | Judges reliability | Records for each source who produced it, how, when, its coverage and its known limitations, and gives a reliability rating with a reason | 11 | Evidence register | ER |
| E4 | Analyses quantitative data | Produces three charts or tables that disaggregate or compare by place, group or time, each with year, source, interpretation and one limitation | 12 | Data worksheet | WP, ER |
| E5 | Uses published qualitative and comparative evidence | Draws themes from published qualitative sources, such as social audit reports, CAG findings, committee testimony and field studies, and builds a comparative matrix of at least two other places that tackled a similar problem, noting the conditions for transfer | 13 | Comparative case matrix; thematic notes | WP |
| E6 | Triangulates | For each main finding, cites at least two independent sources of different types, or states that only one exists | 13, 14 | Testing matrix; register | ER, CR |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Relies on one source or on secondary summaries. Pays little attention to year, coverage or method. Charts lack sources |
| Working | Uses several types of source, rates reliability with reasons, disaggregates data, triangulates the main findings and states limitations |
| Advanced | Identifies where sources conflict and explains why, recognises measurement problems in administrative data, and adjusts confidence in findings accordingly |

### Domain F. Hypothesis-driven analysis

Outcome 5. The habit that separates analysis from advocacy is trying to prove yourself wrong.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Formulates testable hypotheses | Writes three to five hypotheses, each specific enough that evidence could show it to be wrong | 9 | Hypothesis register | WP |
| F2 | Specifies disconfirming evidence | For each hypothesis, states what evidence would weaken it and where that evidence might be found | 9 | Hypothesis register | WP (Gate 2) |
| F3 | Considers rival explanations | For each priority hypothesis, names at least one competing explanation and what evidence would tell the two apart | 9, 14 | Register; testing matrix | WP, PD |
| F4 | Separates fact, inference, assumption and hypothesis | Tags substantive claims in the Ledger correctly in at least nine of ten claims sampled at each gate | 3 onwards | Analyst's Ledger | WP |
| F5 | Updates beliefs on evidence | Records a verdict for each hypothesis (supported, weakened or unresolved) with the evidence, and explains in the reflection how the sealed answer changed | 14, 20 | Testing matrix; reflection | WP, CR |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Hypotheses are vague or restate the problem. Looks only for confirming evidence. Claims are untagged or wrongly tagged |
| Working | Falsifiable hypotheses with disconfirming tests and rivals. Consistent tagging. Verdicts rest on evidence |
| Advanced | Designs the analysis to tell rivals apart efficiently, drops a favoured hypothesis when the evidence requires it, and traces the consequences for the recommendation |

### Domain G. Policy design and appraisal

Outcome 8. The eightfold path governs this domain: alternatives, criteria, projected outcomes, trade-offs, decision.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Generates distinct options | Produces at least three options that differ in mechanism, not only in scale, plus a "do minimum" baseline. At least one changes an instrument and at least one adjusts settings | 16 | Options canvas | WP |
| G2 | Designs from the citizen's experience | Uses a journey map of the affected group, built from published evidence, to show where each option acts, and states the How Might We question each option answers | 16 | Journey map | WP |
| G3 | Appraises against explicit criteria | Builds an options matrix with stated criteria and weights, covering effectiveness, cost, administrative feasibility, political feasibility and distributional effect, with a reason for every score | 17 | Options appraisal | WP, CR |
| G4 | Estimates cost per outcome and tests sensitivity | Gives a rough cost per unit of outcome for each option with assumptions stated, and shows whether the ranking changes under a plausible alternative assumption | 17 | Appraisal worksheet | WP, CR |
| G5 | Applies the public value test | Tests the preferred option against public value, legitimacy and support, and operational capacity, states which is weakest and says how the plan deals with it | 17 | Appraisal; report | CR, PD |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Options are versions of one idea or a wish list. Scores are unexplained numbers. Distribution is ignored |
| Working | Options are genuinely distinct. Criteria are explicit and include distribution. Scores are reasoned, sensitivity is checked and the recommendation follows from the appraisal |
| Advanced | Shows where the recommendation rests on a value judgement rather than evidence, designs a combined or sequenced option where that is better, and states what would change the choice |

### Domain H. Strategy execution

Outcome 9. A recommendation that ignores how it will be delivered is not finished.

| ID | Competency | Indicator at the Working level | Day | Evidence | Assessed in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Builds a theory of change | Links activities, outputs, intermediate outcomes and final outcomes, and marks each causal link as evidenced or assumed | 18 | Theory of change | WP, CR |
| H2 | Assigns decision rights and responsibilities | Uses RAPID for the key decisions or RACI for the main activities, with named roles rather than "the government", and leaves no activity without an accountable owner | 18 | Responsibility matrix | WP |
| H3 | Chooses a delivery approach | States whether the option should be planned in full or built iteratively, with reasons tied to how predictable the context is and how much capability exists | 18 | Implementation plan | WP, CR |
| H4 | Manages risk | Keeps a risk register of at least five risks spanning political, administrative, financial, frontline and distributional risk, each with likelihood, impact, owner and mitigation | 18 | Risk register | WP |
| H5 | Designs indicators | Builds an indicator framework that separates inputs, outputs and outcomes, gives each indicator a source, baseline requirement and frequency, and includes at least one disaggregated indicator | 19 | Indicator framework | WP, CR |

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Foundational | The plan lists activities and dates. The theory of change is a flowchart without assumptions. Indicators measure spending or activity only |
| Working | Theory of change with assumptions marked. Named owners. A reasoned delivery approach. A risk register. Output and outcome indicators with sources |
| Advanced | Links the riskiest assumptions to early indicators and decision points so that the plan can adapt, and anticipates how frontline workers will respond |

Checks:

- Every outcome is assessed in at least two components.
- Every outcome is assessed individually. The only collective element is the 5 per cent pod synthesis within PD.
- Outcome 10 rests mainly on a single event, the Day 20 defence. That is acceptable for an oral skill, but the rubric must be reliable. Stage 8 should provide a structured question bank and a second assessor where possible.
- Diagnosis (Outcomes 1 to 7) is assessed mainly through the workbook portfolio and evidence register, which carry 50 per cent of the marks. Choice and delivery (Outcomes 8 and 9) are assessed mainly through the report and the presentation, which carry 40 per cent. The balance is reasonable, given that diagnosis takes three of the four weeks.

## 9. Cross-cutting professional skills and responsible AI

These skills are not taught in a separate module. They are practised through the case, and each has a standard and an artefact.

| Skill | Where practised | Standard expected | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet analysis | Pre-course primer; Days 12 and 17 | Sorting, filtering, a pivot table, a simple chart, formulas another person can follow | Data worksheet; cost-per-outcome sheet |
| Slide construction | Days 15 and 19 | Claim titles, one message per slide, the Lab's visual template | Deck |
| Analytical writing | Days 2 and 15; report | Top-down memo and executive summary; plain English | Canvas; memo; report |
| Desk research and source management | Days 10 and 11 | Register maintained; Chicago-style citations | Evidence register |
| Peer critique and teamwork | Weekly pod sessions | Rotating roles carried out; specific written reviews | Pod log; peer reviews |
| Time and project management | Day 10 workplan; gates | Gates submitted on time; workplan updated | Workplan; gate submissions |
| Responsible AI use | Throughout | See below | AI disclosure statement at each gate |
| Interviewing | Not taught | Removed by Decision 5 | |

**Responsible AI use.** Participants may use AI tools to suggest branches for an issue tree, propose rival hypotheses, critique a draft argument, find gaps in an analysis and improve the clarity of their writing. They may not use AI output as a source of facts, statistics, citations, legal provisions or descriptions of institutional responsibilities. Every such claim must be traced to a primary or authoritative source and recorded in the evidence register. Each gate submission carries a short disclosure statement describing any AI assistance. AI-generated evidence is not evidence.

## 10. Entry and exit diagnostic

**Task.** Read a two-page case brief. Write a problem statement of no more than 150 words. List the five questions you would most need answered. Suggest one thing the responsible official should do. Time: 45 minutes, completed at home before Day 1 and again in the final week.

**Rubric.** Four criteria, each scored 0 to 3, for a total out of 12.

| Criterion | Linked to | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Precision of the problem statement | A1, A2 | Restates the topic | Names a problem but embeds a cause or solution, or is unbounded | Specific and free of solutions; partly bounded | Specific, bounded, focused on outcomes for people, and names who decides |
| Quality of questions | B1, F1 | Generic | Relevant but overlapping or unordered | Distinct and covering the main areas | Structured, non-overlapping and prioritised |
| Awareness of evidence | E2, E3 | None | Mentions "data" in general | Names plausible specific sources | Names sources and their limits |
| Actionability | A4, D1, G3 | None, or a slogan | An action without an actor | An actor and an action | An actor, an action and a reason it is feasible, or the main risk |

**Design safeguards.** Two case briefs of similar difficulty are used. Half the cohort gets Brief A at entry and Brief B at exit; the other half gets the reverse, so that a difference in difficulty between the briefs is not mistaken for progress. Scripts are anonymised and mixed before scoring, so the assessor does not know whether a script is from entry or exit. The cohort's average change is the programme's first real measure of whether it works.

## 13. Gap audit and next steps

### 13.1 Findings

| Finding | Why it matters | Proposed fix |
|---|---|---|
| Domains G and H get only one formative check (Gate 4) before the summative assessment | Participants meet option appraisal and delivery design for the first time in the final week, and the report is the first place much of that work is marked | Add a pod-reviewed check of the options appraisal on Day 17, with one written comment from the instructor (P-19) |
