# Spirit and Form
## Learning Outcomes and Competency Framework

Version 1.0 · November 2026

## 1. Programme learning outcomes

By the end of the seminar, a participant who meets the completion standard will be able to:

1. **Situate.** Place The Renaissance in India, A Defence of Indian Culture and the plays in their historical and intellectual setting: Sri Aurobindo's life, the Arya (1914 to 1921) and the debate on Indian civilisation from James Mill to William Archer.
2. **Reconstruct.** Reconstruct the main arguments of the essays accurately, as premises and conclusions, with references.
3. **Define.** Explain the key concepts in his sense: spirit and form, svabhava and svadharma, spirituality and religion, renaissance and revival, assimilation and imitation, the four aims of life, spiritual expression in art, and the sovereignty of dharma in polity.
4. **Analyse rhetoric.** Identify how the essays argue: reframing, concession, counter-claim and critique of the critic's standard.
5. **Evaluate evidence.** Distinguish evidence from illustration, and historical claims from normative ideals.
6. **Engage critique.** State the strongest criticisms of the essays (from Archer, Ambedkar, Said, Chatterjee and others) fairly, and weigh them.
7. **Read drama.** Analyse the five complete plays for structure, character, theme, verse and their use of sources.
8. **Connect.** Relate the plays to the essays and both to present debates on culture, tradition and modernity.
9. **Perform.** Speak verse with understanding and stage a scene with a clear interpretation.
10. **Argue.** Write and defend an original, arguable thesis about the texts without overstating the evidence.

## 3. Levels

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Foundational | Summarises the text accurately with support; judgements are reactions rather than arguments; references are sometimes missing. |
| Working | Reconstructs arguments and analyses scenes independently, with references; judges with reasons; states critics fairly. **Completion standard.** |
| Advanced | Moves easily between essays and plays; weighs competing readings; concedes where necessary and still argues; offers a genuinely original thesis. |
