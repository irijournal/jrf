# Spirit and Form
## Participant Handbook

Welcome. For six weeks you will read three bodies of work by Sri Aurobindo: the essays of The Renaissance in India, the essays of A Defence of Indian Culture, and his five complete plays. You will carry one question of your own from the first day to the last, and you will speak his verse aloud. This handbook tells you what to expect and what is expected of you.

## 1. What the course is, and is not

It is a close reading of difficult, important texts, in their setting and in conversation with their critics. It teaches you to understand an argument fully, and then to judge it fairly.

It is not a course in any belief or practice. You are not asked to agree with Sri Aurobindo, or to disagree with him. You are asked to understand him before you judge him, and to give reasons for your judgement.

## 2. The texts

You need The Complete Works of Sri Aurobindo, Volume 20 (The Renaissance in India with A Defence of Indian Culture) and Volumes 3 and 4 (Collected Plays and Stories). The Sri Aurobindo Ashram publishes these, and makes the texts available online. Use one edition throughout and note it in your workbook.

## 3. A typical day

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | An opening question |
| 10 to 35 | Context and concepts |
| 35 to 55 | Close reading of the passage of the day (or, on play days, a read-through) |
| 55 to 90 | A group activity |
| 90 to 110 | Your worksheet: the day's reading linked to your own question |
| 110 to 120 | Debrief and an exit task |

On Fridays (Days 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25) you submit a gate and review a classmate's work. Days 28 to 30 are for performance and the capstone.

## 4. The Reader's Ledger

Every day you keep a Ledger with four columns:

| Text | Inference | Judgement | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the passage says, in your words, with essay or act and page | What it implies or assumes | What you think, and why | What you still need to find out |

Keeping these four apart is the most important habit of the course.

## 5. Workload

| Element | Hours |
|---|---|
| Live sessions, 30 × 2 hours | 60 |
| Reading and worksheets, about an hour on most days | about 30 |
| Gate essays and maps | about 10 |
| Capstone essay and rehearsal | about 15 |
| **Total** | **about 115** |

That is roughly 19 hours a week. Plan for it.

## 6. Gates and the capstone

| Gate | Day | What you submit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Your Week 1 journal, concept cards and a capstone question |
| 2 | 10 | An essay of 1,200 words on one argument from The Renaissance in India |
| 3 | 15 | An argument map and a 300-word response on a Week 3 debate |
| 4 | 20 | A comparative essay of 1,500 words on art, literature or polity |
| 5 | 25 | A close analysis of 1,200 words of one scene from a play |
| Capstone | 30 | Presentation, defence and staged reading; essay due 72 hours later |

Gates receive detailed feedback and are marked at the end as part of your portfolio. Revision is credited: show what you changed and why.

## 7. Norms for discussion

- Argue with texts, not with people. Cite the passage.
- State the views you disagree with at their strongest before answering them.
- Some topics, especially caste, touch people's lives directly. Speak with care; listen with care.
- Disagreement is welcome. Contempt is not.

## 8. Accuracy and AI tools

AI tools invent quotations, page numbers and plot details, especially for less widely read works such as these plays. You may use AI tools to test your structure or improve your prose. You may never use their output as evidence of what any author wrote. Check every quotation against the printed text, and disclose any AI use with each submission. An invented quotation is treated as a breach of academic integrity.

## 9. Performance

Everyone reads verse aloud in the play sessions. On Day 30 your company performs a three-minute scene. You need no acting experience. If you would rather not perform, you may direct or introduce your company's scene; tell the instructor by Day 27.

## 10. Before Day 1

- Obtain the texts.
- Complete the entry diagnostic: a short passage with three questions, 45 minutes.
- Read 'The Renaissance in India', essay I, once through, without notes.
- Think of a question about Indian culture that matters to you.
