# Spirit and Form
## Performance Studio Guide

Every participant speaks Sri Aurobindo's verse aloud and takes part in a staged reading. This guide explains why, and how to run the read-throughs (Days 22 to 27), the performance studio (Day 28), the rehearsal (Day 29) and the staged readings (Day 30). No acting experience is needed.

## 1. Why perform

- Verse drama is written to be heard. Rhythm, pause and stress carry meaning that silent reading misses.
- Staging forces interpretation: every decision about voice and position argues a reading.
- Performing a scene from a play set in Syria or Norway shows, in the body, what the essays argue about cultures meeting.

## 2. Companies

Form companies of five on Day 22, mixing confident and hesitant readers. Each company works on one play from Day 27. Roles rotate within the company: director, two or three readers, and an introducer who links the scene to the essays.

## 3. Choosing a scene

Choose a scene of about three minutes (roughly 40 to 60 lines) with:
- a clear turn: something changes between its first and last line;
- two to four speaking parts;
- a link to a concept from the essays.

Suggested moments, to be located in your edition:

| Play | Suggested moment | Concept from the essays |
|---|---|---|
| Vasavadutta | A music lesson between Udayan and Vasavadutta | Beauty and art as carriers of the spirit |
| The Viziers of Bassora | The turning point of the comedy, or the caliph's judgement | Just rule and good counsel |
| Rodogune | A confrontation between the queen and one of her sons | Tragedy born of fear and ambition |
| Perseus the Deliverer | A debate about the gods and the sacrifice | Spirituality against religiosity |
| Eric | Aslaug's moment of choice | Assimilation of enemies into a larger unity |

Instructors should read the plays and confirm that the chosen moments exist in the form described before assigning them.

## 4. Speaking verse

1. **Follow the sense first.** Read the passage as prose for meaning; mark where each sentence ends, which is often not at a line end.
2. **Then the pulse.** Read it again for rhythm; feel the five beats of the line without thumping them.
3. **Breathe at punctuation, not at line ends.** Carry the sense over line breaks.
4. **Stress the new information.** In each line, one or two words carry what is new; let the rest support them.
5. **Slow down for images.** Give the listener time to see.
6. **Do not declaim.** Speak to the other character, not to the hall.

## 5. From interpretation to staging

| Decision | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Interpretation | What is the scene about, in one sentence? | Enmity becomes intimacy through art |
| Moment | Where is the turn? | The first time the princess plays without correction |
| Voice | How should it be spoken? | Slowly, with pauses after each instruction |
| Space | Where do people stand, and when do they move? | Far apart at first, side by side at the end |

Props, costume and music are unnecessary. A chair, scripts in hand and a clear space are enough.

## 6. Schedule

| Day | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 22 | Read-through of passages from all five plays; companies formed | 35 minutes |
| 23 to 27 | Read-through of the scene of the day in each play session | 25 minutes a day |
| 27 | Companies choose their scene | 10 minutes |
| 28 | Performance studio: interpretation, staging, rehearsal, showing to a partner company | 80 minutes |
| 29 | Timed rehearsal | Part of a 50-minute block |
| 30 | Staged readings: one-minute introduction, three-minute scene | 20 minutes in all |

## 7. Assessment

| Criterion | Foundational | Working | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpretation (company) | No clear reading | A clear one-sentence interpretation that the staging serves | An interpretation that reveals something new in the scene |
| Verse speaking (individual) | Hard to hear or follow | Clear, follows the sense, with some sense of rhythm | Sense and rhythm together; the verse sounds natural and alive |
| Link to the essays (introducer) | Missing or vague | Clear and accurate | Illuminating, and adds to the scene |

## 8. Access and inclusion

Participants who prefer not to perform may direct or introduce, and may submit a written staging plan in place of speaking. Readers with print disabilities receive scripts in accessible formats in advance. Nobody is required to perform in a language or a role that causes distress.
