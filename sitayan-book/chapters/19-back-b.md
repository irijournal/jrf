# Appendix B. The Corpus at a Glance {.unnumbered}

The research corpus on which this book rests holds 243 works, recorded in the author's workbook *Sitayans: Feminist Retellings of the Ramayana* (last updated 10 November 2026). They are listed here by period, with the identifier used in the notes, the creator, the date as recorded, the original language, the form, and a one-line description drawn from the workbook's characterisation field. Dates marked "check" in the workbook remain to be confirmed.


## Classical (before 1000) (7 works)

**W001. *The Ramayana of Valmiki (critical edition translation)*.** Valmiki (attrib.); tr. Robert P. Goldman, Sally J. Sutherland Goldman and others. c. 5th c. BCE to 3rd c. CE; tr. 1984 to 2017; one volume 2021. Sanskrit; epic / long poem; source text. The base the retellings answer. Sita insists on going to the forest and taunts Rama as "a woman with a man's form" when he hesitates; she rebukes him before the fire; in the Uttara Kanda she refuses a second test and returns to the earth.

**W002. *Dasaratha Jataka*.** Pali Buddhist Jataka (anonymous). c. 3rd c. BCE onward. Pali; classical tale / text; source text. Rama, Lakkhana and Sita are siblings; Sita becomes Rama's queen after the exile; no abduction, no war and no fire test.

**W003. *Ramopakhyana (the Rama episode of the Mahabharata)*.** Mahabharata, Vana Parva (anonymous). c. 300 BCE to 300 CE. Sanskrit; classical tale / text; source text. A short telling that ends with the return and coronation: there is no banishment of Sita.

**W004. *Paumacariya (Jain Ramayana)*.** Vimalasuri. c. 1st to 5th c. CE (dates disputed). Maharashtri Prakrit; epic / long poem; source text. After the ordeal Sita takes diksha and becomes a Jain nun, and other women follow her; Kaikeyi does not demand the exile; Ravana is a just king with one weakness.

**W005. *Raghuvamsha, canto 14 (Sita's message)*.** Kalidasa. c. 4th to 5th c. CE. Sanskrit; epic / long poem; source text. The abandoned, pregnant Sita sends Rama a message through Lakshmana: restrained and dignified, but pointed, asking whether this act suits his line.

**W006. *Padmacarita*.** Ravisena. 678 CE. Sanskrit; epic / long poem; source text. A Sanskrit Jain telling that keeps Sita's turn to renunciation after the ordeal.

**W007. *Uttararamacarita*.** Bhavabhuti. c. 8th c. CE. Sanskrit; play / theatre; source text. Rama's grief is centre stage; in Act 3 an unseen Sita watches him mourn while the forest goddess Vasanti reproaches him; the play ends in reunion, not in the earth.


## Medieval and early modern (1000 to 1800) (17 works)

**W008. *Iramavataram (Kamba Ramayanam)*.** Kamban. 12th c.. Tamil; epic / long poem; source text. Rama and Sita fall in love at first sight before the bow test; Surpanakha's approach is expanded; the fire test is staged with great feeling; Ahalya is less guilty than in Valmiki.

**W009. *Adbhuta Ramayana*.** Anonymous (attributed to Valmiki). c. 14th to 15th c.. Sanskrit; epic / long poem; source text. When Rama falls before the thousand-headed Ravana, Sita takes the form of Kali and kills him; Sita is born of Mandodari; the text contains a Sita Sahasranama.

**W237. *Sita's Voice in the Assamese Ramayana: Selected Verses from the Ramayana of Madhava Kandali and the Uttarakanda of Sankaradeva*.** Madhava Kandali, Sankaradeva and Madhavdeva; tr. and ed. Tilottoma Misra. 14th to 15th c. verses; English Jun 2024. Assamese; epic / long poem; source text. Misra translates only the passages where Sita speaks in the Assamese Saptakanda Ramayana; in the Uttarakanda ascribed to Sankaradeva, Sita finally rejects Rama and calls him death incarnate (Yamkal).

**W010. *Adhyatma Ramayana*.** Anonymous (part of the Brahmanda Purana). c. 14th to 15th c.. Sanskrit; epic / long poem; source text. Introduces the shadow Sita: the real Sita waits in the fire while an illusory Sita is abducted, so the fire test simply swaps them back.

**W011. *Krittivasi Ramayan*.** Krittibas Ojha. 15th c.. Bengali; epic / long poem; source text. The most popular Bengali Ramayana: devotional and full of local colour; Stewart and Dimock read a quiet critique of Rama's kingship in it.

**W012. *Bilanka Ramayana*.** Sarala Das (attrib.). 15th c.. Odia; epic / long poem; source text. Sita takes the form of Kali to kill the thousand-headed Ravana of Patala (Bilanka).

**W013. *Ananda Ramayana*.** Anonymous. c. 15th c.. Sanskrit; epic / long poem; source text. A Vaishnava telling that adds many new episodes and rituals; Aklujkar studies its humour.

**W014. *Molla Ramayanam*.** Atukuri Molla. 16th c.. Telugu; epic / long poem; primary. A devout telling in plain Telugu, in six kandas, by a woman from a potter (Kummara) family; it follows Valmiki closely and does not criticise Rama.

**W015. *Chandrabati's Ramayan*.** Chandrabati. 16th c.; English tr. 2020. Bengali; epic / long poem; primary. Opens with Mandodari and Sita's birth and gives the story to Sita; the war is compressed; Sita's twelve-month lament (baromasi) carries the narrative; Rama's sister Kukua tricks Sita into drawing Ravana, which brings her banishment.

**W016. *Ramcharitmanas*.** Tulsidas. 1574 to 1577. Awadhi; epic / long poem; source text. Sita as the ideal devoted wife; the shadow Sita; Rama's harsh words at the fire are cut; Shabari's devotion is honoured; the Sundarkand line "dhol, ganvar, shudra, pashu, nari" remains a flashpoint.

**W017. *The Ramayana of Hamida Banu Begum, Queen Mother of Mughal India*.** Manuscript owned by Hamida Banu Begum; study by John Seyller, Marika Sardar and Audrey Truschke. c. 1594 (manuscript); book 2020. Persian; graphic / visual art; primary. A Persian translation of the Ramayana with 56 paintings, owned by Akbar's mother.

**W018. *Hikayat Seri Rama*.** Anonymous. 13th to 17th c. (written versions). Malay; epic / long poem; source text. Ravana (Maharaja Wana) is more sympathetic than an arrogant Rama; the story lives inside Islamic court culture; the oldest manuscript is in the Bodleian (Laud, 1635).

**W019. *Ramchandrika*.** Keshavdas. 1601. Braj Bhasha; epic / long poem; source text. A courtly Braj Ramayana that shows off metres and figures of speech; Sita appears through the conventions of riti poetry.

**W020. *Dastan-i Ram u Sita (Ramayan-i Masihi)*.** Masih Panipati. 1623 to 1624. Persian; epic / long poem; primary. A Persian masnavi of about 5,300 verses, dedicated to Jahangir, that makes the epic a love story; women such as Kaikeyi and Ravana's sister get more narrative space.

**W021. *Ramayanasara (Sanskrit rendering of the king's Telugu Ramayana)*.** Madhuravani. 17th c. (court of Raghunatha Nayak, r. 1600 to 1634). Sanskrit; epic / long poem; primary. A court poetess renders the king's Telugu Ramayana into Sanskrit in 14 cantos.

**W022. *Vashista Ramayanam*.** Tarigonda Vengamamba. 18th c. (poet 1730 to 1817). Telugu; epic / long poem; primary. A devotional Telugu telling in dwipada couplets by a poet who refused the marks of widowhood.

**W023. *Ramakien*.** Court of King Rama I (version). c. 1797 to 1807 (Rama I version). Thai; epic / long poem; source text. A demoness tricks Sita into drawing Ravana's portrait; it is placed under Rama's bed and leads to her banishment.


## Colonial (1800 to 1947) (19 works)

**W024. *Sita Swayamvar*.** Vishnudas Bhave. 1843. Marathi; play / theatre; primary. Usually counted as the first modern Marathi play: a Ramayana wedding story put on stage.

**W025. *Persian Ramayana of Mohar Singh*.** Mohar Singh. 19th c. (Punjab). Persian; epic / long poem; primary. Sita prays that the fire become a rose garden, as it did for Ibrahim; Trijata compares Rama to Isa (Jesus).

**W026. *Sitar Banabas (The Exile of Sita)*.** Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar. 1860. Bengali; novel; primary. A reformer's prose retelling of Sita's exile, drawn from Bhavabhuti, for a new Bengali reading public.

**W027. *Meghnadbadh Kavya (The Slaying of Meghanada)*.** Michael Madhusudan Dutt. 1861. Bengali; epic / long poem; primary. Sympathy shifts to Lanka; Meghnad's wife Pramila leads a band of women warriors to reach her husband and later dies on his pyre; Sita tells her story to the rakshasi Sarama.

**W238. *Birangana Kabya (The Heroines)*.** Michael Madhusudan Dutt. 1862. Bengali; poetry; primary. Eleven verse letters in which women of myth write to the husbands and lovers who failed them; Kaikeyi writes to Dasharatha. The heroines show anger, desire and grief rather than a goddess's calm.

**W028. *"Sita" (in Ancient Ballads and Legends of Hindustan)*.** Toru Dutt. 1882 (posthumous). English; poetry; primary. Three children listen as their mother tells of Sita's second exile, and all of them weep: the story lives in the home.

**W029. *Kabye Upekshita (The Neglected Women of Poetry)*.** Rabindranath Tagore. c. 1900 (collected in Prachin Sahitya, 1907). Bengali; essay / article; scholarship. Tagore names Urmila as the heroine the poets forgot.

**W030. *Kaviyon ki Urmila Vishayak Udasinta (The Poets' Neglect of Urmila)*.** Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi. 1908 (Saraswati). Hindi; essay / article; scholarship. The editor of Saraswati urges Hindi poets to write about Urmila.

**W031. *Ramayan ka ek Scene (A Scene from the Ramayana)*.** Brij Narayan Chakbast. Early 20th c. (poet 1882 to 1926). Urdu; poetry; primary. Rama takes leave of his mother Kaushalya: a mother's grief in the Urdu elegiac style of soz and marsiya.

**W239. *Tapaswini (Tapasvini)*.** Gangadhar Meher. c. 1912 (check). Odia; epic / long poem; primary. An eleven-canto Odia poem on Sita's life after the banishment, in Valmiki's hermitage, until Lava and Kusha meet Rama; Sita takes up an ascetic life and her grief turns into strength.

**W032. *Lanka Dahan (film)*.** Dadasaheb Phalke (dir.); Anna Salunke as Rama and Sita. 1917. Silent; film / tv; primary. A male actor, Anna Salunke, plays both Rama and Sita.

**W033. *Chintavishtayaya Sita (Sita Immersed in Thought)*.** Kumaran Asan. 1919. Malayalam; poetry; primary. Alone after sending her sons to Rama's court, Sita looks back on her life, judges Rama's choices and moves towards her end with self-respect.

**W034. *Sita Agnipravesam (Sita Enters the Fire)*.** Gudipati Venkata Chalam. 1924. Telugu; play / theatre; primary. Sita is a rebel and a logical thinker: she says Ravana was nobler than Rama because he asked for her love and did not treat her as a machine for heirs, and she walks into Ravana's pyre.

**W035. *Panchavati*.** Maithili Sharan Gupt. 1925. Hindi; poetry; primary. A long poem on the Panchavati episode, including Surpanakha's approach to Lakshmana.

**W036. *Saket*.** Maithili Sharan Gupt. 1931. Hindi; epic / long poem; primary. Urmila's separation fills the epic; in Canto 8 Kaikeyi repents in public at Chitrakoot; the tone is nationalist and devotional.

**W037. *Seeta (film)*.** Debaki Bose (dir.); Durga Khote as Sita, Prithviraj Kapoor as Rama. 1934. Hindi; film / tv; primary. A film of the Uttara Kanda and Sita's exile; the first Indian talkie shown at an international festival (Venice, 1934).

**W038. *Ram Rajya (film)*.** Vijay Bhatt (dir.); Shobhana Samarth as Sita. 1943. Hindi; film / tv; primary. Shobhana Samarth's Sita became the calendar image of the ideal wife; often said to be the only film Gandhi watched.

**W039. *Saapa Vimochanam (Deliverance from the Curse)*.** Pudumaippittan. 1943 (also "Agaligai", 1934). Tamil; short stories; primary. Ahalya, freed from stone, meets Sita and learns of the fire ordeal; she chooses to turn back into stone.

**W040. *Shudra Tapasvi*.** Kuvempu. 1944. Kannada; play / theatre; primary. Rama is made to see the injustice of killing the Shudra ascetic Shambuka.


## Post-independence (1947 to 1990) (37 works)

**W041. *Sri Ramayana Darshanam*.** Kuvempu. Vol. 1 1949; Vol. 2 1957. Kannada; epic / long poem; primary. At the fire ordeal Rama rushes into the flames after Sita and walks out holding her hand.

**W042. *Bhoomikanya Sita*.** Mama (B.V.) Warerkar. Written 1950; published 1955 (some sources 1958). Marathi; play / theatre; primary. Urmila is central and protests the ordeals; Sita returns to the earth; the myth is read through the tiller of the land. Natya Niketan refused to stage it.

**W043. *Ramkatha: Utpatti aur Vikas (The Rama Story: Origins and Growth)*.** Camille Bulcke. 1950 (thesis 1949; 2nd ed. 1962). Hindi; book (scholarship); scholarship. A vast survey of Rama stories across languages and religions, tracing how episodes such as Sita's birth and exile changed.

**W209. *Urmila (Hindi epic poem)*.** Balkrishna Sharma 'Naveen'. Year: check (poet 1897 to 1960). Hindi; epic / long poem; primary. A Hindi epic poem, the poet's major work, centred on Urmila, the wife left behind in Ayodhya.

**W210. *Rama Retold*.** Aubrey Menen. 1954. English; novel; primary. A secular, satirical retelling in which Sita is cleverer than Rama; India banned it in the mid-1950s.

**W044. *Asokavanam*.** Kodavatiganti Kutumba Rao. 1950s. Telugu; short stories; primary. A Telugu story set in the Asoka grove, cited by Volga as part of the rationalist rewriting line in Telugu.

**W045. *The Riddle of Rama and Krishna (Riddles in Hinduism, Appendix I)*.** B.R. Ambedkar. Written 1954 to 1955; published 1987. English; essay / article; primary. Attacks Rama's treatment of Sita (the fire test, the banishment, her refusal of a second test) and of Vali; cites the Buddhist Jataka where Sita is Rama's sister.

**W046. *Ramayana Patirangal / The Ramayana: A True Reading (Hindi: Sachchi Ramayan)*.** E.V. Ramasamy (Periyar); Hindi edition published by Lalai Singh Yadav. English 1959; Hindi 1968; court cases 1969 to 1976. Tamil; essay / article; primary. Reads the Ramayana as Aryan conquest of the Dravidian south: Ravana is the wronged hero and Rama the aggressor; Sita is treated harshly as part of the attack on Rama.

**W211. *Sita Haran (The Abduction of Sita)*.** Qurratulain Hyder. Year: check (1960 or 1968). Urdu; novel; primary. Sita Mirchandani, a Sindhi Hindu refugee after Partition, breaks the rules of respectable society in love and marriage; the title sets the epic against a modern woman's life.

**W047. *Kanchana Sita (play)*.** C.N. Sreekantan Nair. 1961. Malayalam; play / theatre; primary. Sita is absent in body and present as nature, while Rama is tested by his own idea of dharma.

**W212. *Agni-pariksha (Ordeal by Fire)*.** Acharya Tulsi. 1961. Hindi; epic / long poem; primary. A modern Jain telling in Hindi verse with Sita as its main character; it sympathises with her as a woman let down by men and by society.

**W048. *Kathanayika Molla (film)*.** B. Padmanabham (dir.). 1970. Telugu; film / tv; primary. A popular film on the life of the poet Molla.

**W049. *Seetha Josyam*.** Narla Venkateswara Rao. Year: check. Telugu; play / theatre; primary. Sita openly criticises Rama's campaigns against the forest peoples, waged on behalf of the ascetics.

**W050. *When We Dead Awaken: Writing as Re-Vision*.** Adrienne Rich. 1972 (College English). English; essay / article; theory. Defines re-vision as "entering an old text from a new critical direction" as an act of survival for women.

**W213. *Seetha Muthal Sathyavathi Vare (From Sita to Satyavati)*.** Lalithambika Antharjanam. 1972. Malayalam; essay / article; primary. Essays on thirteen women of the Hindu epics, from Sita to Satyavati, by a Nambudiri reformer and pioneer of feminist writing in Malayalam.

**W051. *Ramayana painting (1974) and Mithila Sita imagery*.** Sita Devi (c. 1912 to 2005). 1974. Visual (Mithila painting); graphic / visual art; primary. A leading Mithila painter, Padma Shri 1981, renders the Ramayana in the women's wall-painting tradition of the region where Sita is a daughter.

**W052. *Ramayana Vishavruksham (Ramayana the Poisonous Tree)*.** Ranganayakamma. 1974 to 1976 (3 vols); English 2004. Telugu; short stories; primary. A Marxist rereading built from Valmiki itself: 16 stories, linking essays and 504 footnotes expose class, caste and gender power in the "ideal" kingdom.

**W053. *Where Shall We Go This Summer?*.** Anita Desai. 1975. English; novel; primary. A pregnant, middle-aged woman named Sita leaves her Bombay home for the island of Manori, wishing not to give birth into a violent world.

**W054. *Kanchana Sita (film)*.** G. Aravindan (dir.). Made 1977; released 21 Dec 1978. Malayalam; film / tv; primary. Sita never appears in human form: she is present as nature (Prakriti). Rama Chenchu tribal people play the heroes; Urmila asks whether man can be separate from nature.

**W055. *Nietzsche, Genealogy, History (in Language, Counter-Memory, Practice)*.** Michel Foucault. 1977 (English collection). French; essay / article; theory. Genealogy and counter-memory: history as many descents rather than one origin.

**W056. *The Ramayana Tradition in Asia*.** V. Raghavan (ed.). 1980. English; edited volume / anthology; scholarship. Conference papers on Ramayanas across South and Southeast Asia.

**W057. *The Thieves of Language: Women Poets and Revisionist Mythmaking*.** Alicia Ostriker. 1982 (Signs). English; essay / article; theory. Women poets steal and remake old myths, turning familiar stories to their own ends.

**W058. *The Development of the Sita Myth: A Case Study of Women in Myth and Literature*.** Uma Chakravarti. 1983 (Samya Shakti 1(1): 68 to 75). English; essay / article; scholarship. Traces how Sita was shaped into the model of wifely devotion and how that model served patriarchy over time.

**W059. *"Shabari" (To Shabari)*.** Hira Bansode. Year: check (1980s). Marathi; poetry; primary. A Dalit poet asks Shabari why she offered Rama berries instead of asking about Eklavya's thumb and Sita's exile.

**W060. *Ramayan (TV serial)*.** Ramanand Sagar (creator); Doordarshan. 1987 to 1988. Hindi; film / tv; source text. The Doordarshan serial that fixed a pan-Indian image of a calm, obedient Sita; Sunday mornings emptied the streets.

**W061. *Sitayana: Epic of the Earth-born*.** K.R. Srinivasa Iyengar. Begun 1983; published c. 1987 (check). English; epic / long poem; primary. An English epic of Sita's life as daughter of the earth.

**W062. *Three Hundred Ramayanas: Five Examples and Three Thoughts on Translation*.** A.K. Ramanujan. 1987 (talk); 1991 (in Many Ramayanas); removed from the DU syllabus 2011. English; essay / article; scholarship. Argues there is no single original Ramayana, only a common pool of tellings; compares episodes such as Ahalya's across languages.

**W240. *Three Indian Princesses: The Stories of Savitri, Damayanti and Sita*.** Jamila Gavin (illustrated by Govinder Ram). 1987. English; children's book; primary. Three legends retold for children of about 7 to 9; in the Sita story, the loyal wife follows Rama into exile and is carried off by Ravana.

**W063. *Bargaining with Patriarchy*.** Deniz Kandiyoti. 1988 (Gender and Society). English; essay / article; theory. Women strategise within the limits of a given patriarchy; older women can gain power by enforcing its rules.

**W064. *Can the Subaltern Speak?*.** Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak. 1988. English; essay / article; theory. Asks whether the subaltern woman can be heard when others speak for her; sati is the central case.

**W065. *Staying Alive: Women, Ecology and Survival in India*.** Vandana Shiva. 1988. English; book (scholarship); theory. Links women, nature and the feminine principle (Prakriti) against Western development.

**W066. *Recasting Women: Essays in Colonial History*.** Kumkum Sangari and Sudesh Vaid (eds). 1989. English; edited volume / anthology; theory. Essays on reform, nationalism and gender, including Partha Chatterjee's "The Nationalist Resolution of the Women's Question".

**W067. *Sita and Draupadi: Aggressive Behavior and Female Role-Models in the Sanskrit Epics*.** Sally J. Sutherland. 1989 (JAOS 109(1): 63 to 79). English; essay / article; scholarship. Contrasts the passive, self-directed aggression of Sita with Draupadi's open anger as two models for women.

**W068. *The Perfect Wife: The Orthodox Hindu Woman according to the Stridharmapaddhati*.** Julia Leslie. 1989. English; book (scholarship); theory. Studies an 18th-century Sanskrit manual on the duties of wives.

**W069. *The Ramayana Syndrome*.** Romila Thapar. 1989 (Seminar 353). English; essay / article; scholarship. Warns that the TV serial turned many Ramayanas into one official version.

**W070. *Ramayan: The Video*.** Philip Lutgendorf. 1990 (The Drama Review). English; essay / article; scholarship. Studies the making and devotional viewing of Sagar's serial.

**W071. *Sita's Daughters (dance-theatre)*.** Mallika Sarabhai (with John Martin). 1990. Hindi; English; Gujarati; dance / performance; primary. A performance about women who refuse patriarchy, staged more than 500 times; it drew right-wing backlash.


## Liberalisation era (1991 to 2009) (64 works)

**W072. *A Ramayana of Their Own: Women's Oral Tradition in Telugu*.** Velcheru Narayana Rao. 1991 (Many Ramayanas). English; essay / article; scholarship. Telugu women's songs dwell on pregnancy, birth, marriage and household quarrels rather than war: a separate women's Ramayana.

**W073. *Fire and Flood: The Testing of Sita in Kampan's Iramavataram*.** David Shulman (check). 1991 (Many Ramayanas, 89 to 113). English; essay / article; scholarship. A close reading of the fire test in Kamban.

**W074. *Many Ramayanas: The Diversity of a Narrative Tradition in South Asia*.** Paula Richman (ed.). 1991. English; edited volume / anthology; scholarship. Essays on Ramayanas across languages, religions and castes: Ramanujan, Erndl on Surpanakha, V.N. Rao on Telugu women's songs, Richman on Periyar, Lamb on the Ramnamis.

**W075. *The Mutilation of Surpanakha*.** Kathleen M. Erndl. 1991 (Many Ramayanas, 67 to 88). English; essay / article; scholarship. Compares the episode in Valmiki, Kamban, the Adhyatma, Tulsidas and modern Hindi verse; Sita is the chaste good woman and Surpanakha the "loose" bad woman.

**W076. *The Woman as Communal Subject: Rashtrasevika Samiti and Ram Janmabhoomi Movement*.** Tanika Sarkar. 1991 (EPW). English; essay / article; scholarship. Shows how Hindu nationalist women's organisations took part in the Ayodhya movement and used ideals of womanhood.

**W077. *Doosra Banbas (The Second Exile)*.** Kaifi Azmi. 1992 (after 6 December). Urdu; poetry; primary. Rama comes home from exile, sees blood on the banks of the Sarju, rises without washing his feet and leaves: "on 6 December I got a second exile".

**W078. *The Gender and Environment Debate: Lessons from India*.** Bina Agarwal. 1992 (Feminist Studies). English; essay / article; theory. Criticises ecofeminism that treats women and nature as one; proposes a material, class-based "feminist environmentalism".

**W079. *Conceptualising Brahmanical Patriarchy in Early India: Gender, Caste, Class and State*.** Uma Chakravarti. 1993 (EPW 28(14)). English; essay / article; theory. Shows how control of women's sexuality kept caste purity and class power in place.

**W080. *Deity to Crusader: The Changing Iconography of Ram*.** Anuradha Kapur. 1993 (in Hindus and Others, ed. Gyanendra Pandey). English; essay / article; scholarship. Tracks how Rama's image shifted from gentle deity to aggressive warrior in Ayodhya-movement posters.

**W081. *Ecofeminism*.** Maria Mies and Vandana Shiva. 1993. English; book (scholarship); theory. A joint case for ecofeminism against global capitalism.

**W082. *Ramayana and Political Imagination in India*.** Sheldon Pollock. 1993 (Journal of Asian Studies). English; essay / article; scholarship. Argues the Ramayana became a political text for kings, especially in times of conflict, from the 12th century onward.

**W083. *We Make It Better (review of Many Ramayanas)*.** Ruth Vanita. c. 1993 (Manushi 78). English; essay / article; scholarship. Criticises predictable feminist readings, including Erndl's, and questions romanticising "people's" versions.

**W084. *The Legend of Rama: Artistic Visions*.** Vidya Dehejia (ed.). 1994 (Marg). English; edited volume / anthology; scholarship. Essays on the Ramayana in Indian painting and sculpture.

**W085. *Kishwar on Shetkari Sanghatana's "Lakshmi Mukti" land campaign*.** Madhu Kishwar. Year: check. English; essay / article; scholarship. Discusses a farmers' campaign for land in women's names and its use of Sita imagery.

**W086. *Sita's songs of love, exile and sorrow (Grindmill Songs Project)*.** Women singers of rural Maharashtra; PARI Grindmill Songs team (Namita Waikar and others). Oral tradition; recorded 1990s; published 25 Jan 2022. Marathi; folk songs (oral); primary. In grindmill songs (ovi), jujube and acacia trees act like women who console Sita in the forest; singers also mourn Sulochana.

**W087. *Women and the Hindu Right: A Collection of Essays*.** Tanika Sarkar and Urvashi Butalia (eds). 1995. English; edited volume / anthology; scholarship. Essays on women's place in Hindu nationalist politics.

**W088. *Sita Theke Shuru (Beginning from Sita)*.** Nabaneeta Dev Sen. 1996. Bengali; essay / article; scholarship. Bengali essays on women retelling the Ramayana, the ground for her English lectures.

**W214. *Sitayan*.** Mallika Sengupta. Nov 1996. Bengali; novel; primary. Sita tells the Uttara Kanda herself, from the day Lakshmana leaves her near Valmiki's ashram to her departure in a mysterious chariot; Kaushalya takes her side; Ravana questions Aryan ethics; the killing of Shambuka is part of the story.

**W089. *Mahamoha (Ahalya: A Woman's Eternal Quest for Love)*.** Pratibha Ray. 1997 to 1998 (often cited as 2012). Odia; novel; primary. Retells Ahalya as a woman's search for love; the English title calls it an "eternal quest for love".

**W090. *Rewriting the Ramayana: Chandrabati and Molla*.** Nabaneeta Dev Sen. 1997. English; essay / article; scholarship. Compares two 16th-century women poets: Molla, who chose Telugu but kept a devout, uncritical telling, and Chandrabati, who skipped the war and told the story through Sita's lament.

**W091. *Yes to Sita, No to Ram! The Continuing Popularity of Sita in India*.** Madhu Kishwar. 1997 (Manushi 98: 20 to 31); reprinted 2001. English; essay / article; scholarship. Most poems sent to Hindi Manushi were about Sita; women admire Sita but do not want a husband like Rama, and see her dharma as higher than his.

**W092. *The Sita Symposium (Columbia University)*.** Moderated by J.S. Hawley; papers by Linda Hess, Tom Coburn, J.E. Llewellyn, Phyllis Herman, Nabaneeta Dev Sen. April 1998. English; essay / article; scholarship. A one-day meeting whose papers include "Once Upon a Time... Sita Slew Ravana" (Coburn), "The Arya Sita and the Power of Self-Sacrifice" (Llewellyn) and "Relocating Ramarajya: Sita's Kitchen in Ayodhya" (Herman).

**W093. *When Women Retell the Ramayan (also "Lady Sings the Blues")*.** Nabaneeta Dev Sen. 1998 (Manushi 108). English; essay / article; scholarship. Four ways women retell (borrowing male eyes, using women's own eyes, an ideological lens, and more); women's songs favour the Bala and Uttara Kandas; village women take the Sita myth "to give themselves a voice".

**W094. *Rejecting Sita: Indian Responses to the Ideal Man's Cruel Treatment of His Ideal Wife*.** Linda Hess. 1999 (JAAR 67(1): 1 to 32). English; essay / article; scholarship. Gathers how audiences, performers and writers respond to Rama's rejection of Sita, from discomfort to open protest.
