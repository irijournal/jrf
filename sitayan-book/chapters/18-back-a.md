# Glossary of Sanskrit and Regional Terms {.unnumbered}

**agni-parīkṣā** — "ordeal by fire"; Sita's entry into the fire after the war (Vālmīki 6.104).

**anīśvarā** — "without sovereignty, not the mistress"; Sita's word for herself in Laṅkā with respect to her body (6.104.9).

**antharjanam** (Malayalam) — "one who lives within"; a Nambūtiri Brahmin woman, secluded in the inner quarters.

**apavāda** — public censure, disgrace; what Rama fears (6.103.16; 7.44.13).

**āśrama** — hermitage; Vālmīki's, where Sita is received after the banishment.

**asatī** — "unchaste", the negation of *satī*; Rama's word for Śūrpaṇakhā (3.17.20).

**bāromāsī** (Bengali) — twelve-month song; the women's lyric form that carries Candrābatī's narrative of Sita's captivity.

**bhakti** — devotion; the devotional mode of the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* and Tulsīdās.

***deśaj*** (Hindi) — "born of the land"; in Hindi grammar, a word of native origin, neither Sanskrit (*tatsam*), derived from Sanskrit (*tadbhav*), nor foreign (*videśaj*). Anamika's term for her feminism.

**dharma** — duty, right conduct, law; the principle in whose name Rama acts and against which Sita measures him.

**dharmaśāstra** — the normative literature on law and conduct.

**divya** — "divine proof"; an ordeal used in *dharmaśāstra* when human evidence is wanting.

**dīkṣā** — initiation; the Jain Sita's renunciation in the *Paumacariya*.

**dhobī** — washerman; in later tellings, the man whose remark prompts the banishment.

**janam-dukhinī** (Bengali, Maithili) — "born to suffer"; the commonest epithet for Sita in women's songs (Dev Sen, *Manushi* 108, p. 21).

**kāṣāyavāsinī** — "dressed in ochre"; Sita at the final assembly (7.88.9).

**kanyā** — maiden; the five *kanyās* (Ahalyā, Draupadī, Kuntī, Tārā, Mandodarī) of devotional recitation.

**kīrti, yaśas** — fame, reputation; what Rama seeks to protect.

**lokasya sākṣī** — "witness of the world"; Sita's name for the fire (6.104.24).

**madadhīnam** — "that which depends on me"; Sita's word for her heart (6.104.9).

**māyā-sītā, chāyā-sītā** — the illusory or shadow Sita of the *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa* and the *Mānas*.

**naralīlā** — the god's "human play"; Tulsī's frame for Rama's conduct.

**ovī** (Marathi) — the couplet form of the grindmill songs.

**paṭa, Paṭuā** (Bengali) — scroll; the hereditary painters who sing as they unroll it.

**pativratā** — the wife devoted to her husband as a vow; the ideal against which Sita is measured and which she inhabits.

**pauruṣa** — manliness, manhood; what Rama claims to have vindicated (6.103.2–6).

**prākṛta** — common, vulgar; Sita's charge against Rama's speech (6.104.5).

**pratyaya** — conviction, public belief; Rama's stated reason for permitting the ordeal (6.106.14).

**Rāmrājya** — the reign of Rama; in modern usage, the ideal polity.

**rākṣasa, rākṣasī** — the beings of Laṅkā and the forest; in anti-caste readings, the indigenous peoples.

**sākṣin** — witness; a human means of proof in *dharmaśāstra*.

**sāsurvās** (Marathi) — life in the in-laws' house; a category of women's songs.

**satī** — the faithful wife; also, the woman who dies on her husband's pyre.

**smārtavicāram** (Malayalam) — the caste tribunal that tried a Nambūtiri woman for adultery.

**strīdharma** — the duty of women.

**tapas, tapasvinī** — ascetic austerity; a female ascetic (Gangadhar Meher's title).

**tṛṇa, tṛn dhari oṭ** — blade of grass; "holding a blade of grass as a screen" (Tulsī), the source of Anamika's title.

**Uttarakāṇḍa** — the seventh and last book of the Rāmāyaṇa, containing the banishment and the descent into the earth.

# Appendix A. The Verified Quotation Archive {.unnumbered}

The research plan asked for a quotation archive in which each important passage is recorded with its exact locator, its context, its literary technique, its feminist significance, its theoretical connection, a counter-reading, and its place in the book.^[Research plan, §5.] The entries below are those verified against the source while writing. Sanskrit is from the critical edition as transcribed in GRETIL; translations are mine.

**A1. Vālmīki 2.27.3.** *kiṃ tvāmanyata vaidehaḥ pitā me mithilādhipaḥ / rāma jāmātaraṃ prāpya striyaṃ puruṣavigraham*. "What did my father think he was getting, Rama, when he took as son-in-law a woman in a man's form?" *Context:* Rama has told Sita to stay in Ayodhya. *Technique:* sarcasm, introduced by the poet as a taunt (*paricikṣepa*, 2.27.2). *Significance:* the first time Sita opposes her husband, she prevails by insult. *Theory:* Mahmood: agency inside the idiom of devotion. *Counter-reading:* the taunt is lover's teasing (*praṇayāt*), not defiance. *Chapter:* 1.

**A2. Vālmīki 2.24.8.** *nāsmi saṃprati vaktavyā vartitavyaṃ yathā mayā*. "I need not now be told how I am to behave." *Context:* Sita's first speech on the exile. *Significance:* *strīdharma* invoked to refuse a husband's command. *Chapter:* 1.

**A3. Vālmīki 3.54.1; 5.19.3.** *tṛṇam antarataḥ kṛtvā*. "Placing a blade of grass between them." *Context:* Sita answers Ravana, "fearless though worn down by grief". *Technique:* gesture as boundary. *Significance:* the source of Anamika's title. *Counter-reading:* ritual avoidance of a man's direct address, not defiance. *Chapters:* 1, 14.

**A4. Vālmīki 5.35.62.** *bhartur bhaktiṃ puraskṛtya rāmād anyasya vānara / nāhaṃ spraṣṭuṃ padā gātram iccheyaṃ vānarottama*. "I would not wish to touch, even with my foot, the body of anyone other than Rama." *Significance:* fidelity as choice (desiderative verb). *Counter-reading:* internalised purity norm. *Chapter:* 1.

**A5. Vālmīki 6.103.15–17.** *na tvadarthaṃ mayā kṛtaḥ ... dīpo netrāturasyeva pratikūlāsi me dṛḍham*. "This war was not undertaken for your sake ... you are as hateful to me as a lamp to one with diseased eyes." *Context:* Rama's public charge. *Significance:* the trial protects lineage and reputation. *Theory:* the gendered distribution of proof (Figure 1.3). *Chapter:* 1.

**A6. Vālmīki 6.104.5, 7–9, 14.** "Like a common man to a common woman"; "because of some women you suspect the whole sex"; "that which is in my control, my heart, abides in you; what could I, powerless, do about my limbs in another's control?"; "like a petty man you have put my womanhood alone in front." *Technique:* point-by-point rebuttal; juridical vocabulary (*parīkṣitā*, *pramāṇīkṛta*). *Significance:* the defence becomes a counter-indictment. *Chapter:* 1.

**A7. Vālmīki 6.104.24.** *tathā lokasya sākṣī māṃ sarvataḥ pātu pāvakaḥ*. "So may the purifier, the witness of the world, protect me." *Significance:* the ordeal converted into testimony. *Chapters:* 1, 9.

**A8. Vālmīki 6.106.9, 14.** "You are to say nothing against her. I command you"; "for the sake of the conviction of the three worlds ... I ignored Vaidehī as she entered the fire." *Significance:* the fire god commands the king; the king confesses the trial was a performance. *Chapter:* 1.

**A9. Vālmīki 7.42.19.** *asmākam api dāreṣu sahanīyaṃ bhaviṣyati / yathā hi kurute rājā prajā tam anuvartate*. "Now we too shall have to put up with this in our wives; as the king does, so the subjects follow." *Significance:* the banishment as a ruling on precedent. *Chapters:* 1, 13, 15.

**A10. Vālmīki 7.44.18.** *na cāsmi prativaktavyaḥ sītāṃ prati kathaṃ cana*. "I am not to be answered back concerning Sita in any way." *Significance:* condemnation without hearing or advocacy. *Chapters:* 1, 5.

**A11. Vālmīki 7.47.11–12.** "Treat the citizens always as you treat your brothers ... I do not grieve for my own body." *Significance:* the banished wife instructs the king in kingship. *Chapter:* 1.

**A12. Vālmīki 7.88.9–13.** Sita *kāṣāyavāsinī*; the oath; the Earth "took Maithilī in her arms, greeted her with a welcome, and seated her on the throne". *Significance:* refusal as homecoming. *Counter-reading:* death under pressure (Afterword, Objection 2). *Chapters:* Introduction, 1, Conclusion.

**A13. Vālmīki 1.47.19, 29.** Ahalya consents "out of curiosity about the king of the gods"; the curse makes her invisible, living on air. *Significance:* no stone in Vālmīki. *Chapter:* 6.

**A14. Vālmīki 3.17.20.** *imāṃ virūpām asatīm atimattāṃ mahodarīm*. Rama on Śūrpaṇakhā. *Significance:* the anti-Sita defined. *Chapter:* 10.

**A15. Vālmīki 7.67.2–4.** Śambūka: "I was born from a Śūdra womb"; Rama beheads him while he speaks. *Significance:* the *Uttarakāṇḍa*'s other victim. *Chapters:* 1, 5, 10.

**A16. Dev Sen, *Manushi* 108 (1998), p. 19.** "If patriarchy has used the Sita myth to silence women, the village women have picked up the Sita myth to give themselves a voice." *Chapters:* 2, 7.

**A17. Dev Sen, p. 25.** "Ram is reading about Sita's exile / In a book ..." (Marathi song). *Significance:* the official record held by the one who did not live it. *Chapters:* 2, 9.

**A18. Dev Sen, p. 26.** "Sita's exile, / Let us share it among ourselves ... Sita distributed it amongst us all / Bit by bit." (Marathi song). *Significance:* choral agency. *Chapters:* 2, 8.

**A19. Dev Sen, p. 27.** "When women retell the Ramayan, Sita is the name they give themselves: the homeless female, the foundling, unloved, rejected and insecure." *Chapters:* front matter, 7, 13.

**A20. Kishwar, *Manushi* 98 (1997), p. 23.** The refusal of the second ordeal "interpreted not as an act of self annihilation but as a momentous but dignified rejection of Ram as a husband." *Chapters:* 1, 7.

**A21. Kishwar, p. 24.** "Ram may not have rejected her as a wife but only as a queen in deference to social opinion, but Sita rejects him as a husband." *Chapters:* 1, 8.

**A22. Kishwar, p. 24.** Avadh women's song: "The Ram who put me in the fire, who threw me out of the house / Guru, how shall I see his face?" *Chapter:* 2.

**A23. Kishwar, pp. 26–27.** Sharad Joshi: "how do we men treat our Lakshmis? Often no better than Ram treated Sita"; land as "a long overdue debt" to Sita *mātā*. *Chapter:* 6.

**A24. Kishwar, p. 29.** "a Ram-like son, a Ram-like brother, or a Ram-like king ... never ... a Ram-like husband"; viewers' letters forced Sagar to make the ordeal "a mock one"; Rama "was being ordered to behave better". *Chapters:* 7, 15, Conclusion.

**A25. Kishwar, p. 30.** "The Hindu tradition might valourise wives who put up with tyrannical husbands gracefully but it does not valourise unreasonable husbands." *Chapter:* 7.
